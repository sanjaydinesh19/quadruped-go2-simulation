The policy

235 numbers in, 12 joint targets out.

An actor–critic MLP. Every 20 ms the actor reads the robot's proprioception plus a 187-point height scan of the terrain ahead, and emits target positions for the twelve leg joints. A PD controller turns those into torques at 200 Hz.

Reward design

Two terms ask for motion. Ten keep it honest.

This is where the gait actually comes from. Velocity tracking supplies the goal; every other term is a penalty that rules out a cheap way to achieve it — hopping, scrambling, dragging a foot, or burning the motors to do it.

Future implementation

Where this goes next.

Asymmetric actor–critic

The critic currently sees exactly what the actor sees. Feeding it privileged simulator state — true friction, contact forces, terrain height — sharpens the value estimate at no deployment cost.

Domain randomisation

Randomise mass, friction, motor strength and latency, then measure survival under held-out perturbations. This is the gap between a policy that works in sim and one that survives contact with a real floor.

Sim-to-real transfer

Export to TorchScript and run on physical Go2 hardware. The observation vector is already deployable except for the height scan, which needs a real depth camera.

Teacher–student distillation

Distil this height-scan policy into a blind student that walks on proprioception alone — the standard route to robust hardware locomotion.

Harder curriculum

The hard preset is stiffer than anything in the training mix, and the footage shows it. Training directly on it should close that gap.

Live training console

The upstream repo ships a FastAPI + React dashboard for launching runs and streaming reward curves in the browser. Wiring it up turns this from a result into a platform.
